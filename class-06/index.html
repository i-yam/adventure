Somewhere around month eight the playbook runs out. The problem in front of you is not in any book, and the people who wrote the books are not answering.

Two of the four parts below are about deciding when nothing tells you how. The other two are about the decision founders duck longest, which is what to charge. A price is the shortest sentence a company ever writes. Most founders spend longer on the logo.

Work through them in order. Part 2 uses the page you write in Part 1, and Part 4 needs the field notes from Part 3.

Deep dive: why pricing lives in a class about hard problems optional

Pricing looks like arithmetic. Every method for doing it well runs out before it reaches your number. Cost-plus gives you a floor. Matching a competitor tells you what somebody else guessed. Asking people what they would pay tells you what they say when nothing is at stake, which is a different thing from what they do with a card in their hand.

So at some point you name a figure on incomplete information and live with what happens. That is the same shape as everything else in this class, which is why the two halves sit together instead of the price material waiting for the spreadsheets in Class 09.
