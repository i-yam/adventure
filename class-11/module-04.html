Answer the question they asked

11
INTEREST fundraising / COMFORT no-code / TIME 15min / TRACK core / FEEDS class 12 pitch
The
answer

An investor asks how you get your first thousand customers. You say the market is growing fast and there has been a lot of inbound interest. You have answered a different question, and everyone in the room heard you do it.

Graham's argument in the essay is that investors are trying to work out one thing, whether you are formidable, and that the only reliable way to seem formidable is to be it. The tell is small and it is behavioural. Formidable people put the answer in the first sentence, and they say they do not know when they do not know.

Your deck is built. Class 09 gave you the runway number, Class 10 gave you the hiring line, this class gave you the cap table. Class 12 is the room where somebody with money asks about all three.

The work
[ NO AI ] DO THIS ONE YOURSELF

Sit opposite a partner with your deck closed. They ask ten questions and you answer each one out loud in a single sentence. How do you get the first thousand customers. What does it cost you to get one. How long does the money last. What happens when a company twenty times your size ships this feature. Who on your team quits first, and what breaks when they do. Why now, and why has nobody done it already.

Your partner marks each answer as answered, dodged, or admitted. An answer that opens with context is a dodge. An answer that arrives at the number after three sentences of setup is a dodge with better manners. Admitted is a real category and it costs you nothing, as long as what follows is how you would find out.

Take every dodge and rewrite it flat, starting with the answer. Then open the deck and cut the slide, the sentence, or the chart that was standing in for the answer you did not have.

Three questions will survive this with no answer. Write those three on their own sheet and bring it to Class 12. Being default alive with a number and three honest gaps beats a smooth story with a hole under it, and the room will find the hole in about ninety seconds.

AI off. A model will write you a fluent answer to a question you cannot actually answer, and in Class 12 somebody asks the follow-up.

Reading
Paul Graham · How to Convince Investors

It reads like the rubric for Class 12, because it mostly is. Read it before you pitch.

Deep dive: where the essay stops optional

Graham is writing from inside a system where a small number of people decide fast on very little information, and in that system how you come across in twenty minutes carries real weight. If you are building something that does not need venture money, the same performance is worth much less, and the customer who pays you does not care whether you seemed formidable in a meeting.

There is also a version of this advice that goes wrong. Investors mistake confidence for competence often enough that founders learn to supply confidence, which is how a market ends up funding the most fluent person in the room. Graham's own escape hatch is the honest one. Be default alive, know your numbers, and then the confidence is just a description of something true.
