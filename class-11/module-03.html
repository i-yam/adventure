CLASS 11 · MODULE 03 · BACKPRESSURE

The backlog is lying to you

11
INTEREST ops / COMFORT no-code / TIME 15min / TRACK core / FEEDS class 12 pitch
The
queue

Forty tickets arrive a day. You close thirty.

Nobody is behind on the first morning. The queue gains ten a day and never gives them back. Two weeks in there are 140 waiting, and at thirty closed a day the ticket that arrives now gets looked at in just under five days. Nothing broke. Nobody was lazy. The arithmetic did it while everyone was working hard.

TCP has a solution for this that companies mostly do not. When a link is swamped the receiver tells the sender to slow down, and the sender slows down. That is backpressure, and the reason it works is that the signal travels backwards, towards whoever is causing the load.

The failure mode has a name too. A big buffer looks like resilience, so people add capacity to the queue instead of the work. Everything still arrives, nothing gets dropped, and every item now waits five days. Buffer bloat is the backlog that hides how far behind you already are.

The work
[ AI OK ] USE THE TOOLS

Pick a real queue. Support tickets, sales leads, candidate applications, your own inbox, the product backlog. Count two numbers over the last week, how many arrived and how many you finished. Then divide the current backlog by what you finish in a day. That is the wait a new arrival is looking at, and it is almost always worse than anyone in the room would have guessed out loud.

Now hand the backlog to a model and watch it invent urgency. A prompt that works:

Here is my support backlog, one item per line, with the date it arrived and the customer's plan. Sort it by what I should work on today. For each item, name the piece of information in that line that drove its position. If a line does not tell you how urgent it is, say so instead of guessing.

The last sentence is the whole exercise. Without it you get a confident ordering built on deadlines the model made up, and it reads exactly like the one built on real ones. Count how many items it ranked on information that was actually in the line, and how many it ranked on a story.

Then write the backpressure rule, one sentence, with a number in it. At what backlog size do you stop accepting new work, close a channel, turn off the signup form, or tell the team the next request goes to a list nobody promises anything about. Naming the number now is easier than naming it at 140.

AI on. Triage is a real use, and the model's habit of manufacturing urgency is the thing worth seeing while the stakes are a support queue.

Deep dive: the queue you cannot see optional

Class 06 had scheduling and thrashing, where switching between tasks eats the capacity that was supposed to do them. Backpressure is the same problem seen from the other end of the pipe. Thrashing is what a person does when the queue arrives faster than they can serve it and nobody upstream has been told.

The company version hides in places that do not look like queues. A roadmap with two hundred items on it. A hiring pipeline where forty people are waiting on one interviewer. A founder who is the approval step for six decisions a day. Each of these is a buffer, each is quietly adding latency to everything behind it, and none of them shows up in a metric anybody reports.
