Write the rule, then break it

11
INTEREST ops / COMFORT no-code / TIME 45min / TRACK core / FEEDS class 12 pitch
The
rule

The target was eight products per household. Wells Fargo called it Gr-eight, put it in front of branch staff for years, and never reached it.

What it reached instead was around 3.5 million accounts opened without permission, about 5,300 people fired, $185 million in fines in September 2016, and a chief executive in front of the Senate. The fines split three ways, a hundred million to the consumer bureau, fifty to Los Angeles, thirty-five to the comptroller of the currency.

Almost nobody in that story arrived at work wanting to commit fraud. They wanted the bonus. The bonus sat on a number, the number was reachable if you opened accounts for people who had not asked for them, and thousands of ordinary people found the same door because the rule left it open and paid them to walk through.

You are about to write rules like that one.

The work
[ NO AI ] DO THIS ONE YOURSELF

Write one incentive your company will actually use, in one sentence, with the payout in it. Sales commission, a referral bounty, a creator payout, a bonus for closing tickets, whatever your business runs on. An incentive without a number is a mood, so put the number in.

Now hand it to somebody else and give them fifteen minutes to be the person who wants the money and does not care about you. Their job is the cheapest path to the maximum payout. They are allowed to be shameless. They are not allowed to break the rule, only to follow it exactly.

Take the exploit back and rewrite the rule so the cheap path is closed. Then write the cost of your fix in one line, because every fix has one. Usually it is a cap, a clawback, or a measurement somebody now has to make, and measurement costs time you were not planning to spend.

Last, look at the rule from outside. Uber and Lyft spent years bidding driver bonuses against each other, and both sides played correctly, and the drivers ran both apps and switched at the lights. Ask whether your incentive only works if a competitor declines to copy it. If it does, you have written a rule for a game you cannot win by playing better.

You leave with four lines. The rule, the exploit, the rewrite, and what the rewrite costs. Goodhart showed up in Class 09 as a metric that went bad once you aimed at it. Attach money and it goes bad faster.

AI off. The exploit has to come from a person who knows your company well enough to be genuinely greedy inside it.

Deep dive: leaving the table optional

Some games do not have a winning line. Two companies subsidising the same customer will both keep subsidising until one runs out of money, and playing harder makes the ending arrive sooner. Uber left China in 2016 by folding the business into Didi and taking a stake in the winner, which is one way to stop losing a price war without ever winning it.

The move to look for is a change in the rules rather than a change in effort. Exclusivity, a switching cost, a contract structure, an alliance, a regulator. Founders reach for effort first because effort is the thing they control, and it is the one input the other side can always match.
