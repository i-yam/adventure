Somebody in the room has to be allowed to say no

10
INTEREST ops / COMFORT no-code / TIME 45min / TRACK core / FEEDS class 12 pitch
The
bar

At Amazon, someone from a team you will never work on sits in your interview loop and can kill the hire. They have no seat to fill and no deadline riding on you. They are there to say no.

Amazon calls that person the bar raiser. The idea comes from a simple observation about who is doing the judging. A manager with an empty seat has been covering that seat for two months. They are tired. Every candidate starts to look acceptable around week nine. That manager is the last person who should decide whether the candidate clears the bar, and they are usually the only person who does.

Bezos wrote the standard into the 1998 shareholder letter, in the form of a question. Will this person raise the average effectiveness of the group they are joining. If the answer is no, the hire lowers the bar, and the next candidate gets measured against the lower one. Do that four times and you have a team nobody would join on purpose.

Horowitz gets to the same place from the other side. Hire for strength, not for the absence of weakness. The candidate everyone in the room agrees on is usually the one with no obvious flaws and nothing decisive either. The hire that works is the one with a strength that settles the thing you are actually bad at, and weaknesses you saw coming and accepted with your eyes open.

Netflix learned it by subtraction. In 2001 they cut about a third of the company and expected the remaining people to buckle. The team got faster. Everything Hastings and McCord wrote afterward came out of that surprise, and the surprise was about who stayed.

The work
[ NO AI ] DO THIS ONE YOURSELF

Write the job as one sentence describing the thing that has to go right in the next six months. No title, no seniority, no list of responsibilities. If you cannot finish the sentence, you do not have a role yet, you have a feeling of being busy.

Name the single strength that decides that sentence. One. Then write the test that would show it inside an hour, and make it work rather than conversation. A person who can do the thing can do it in front of you.

Write the two weaknesses you are agreeing to live with. In writing, dated. You are going to discover them in month three either way, and the only question is whether you knew.

Set the bar. Name the person on your team, or in this room, that the hire has to be better than at that one strength. If you cannot name anyone, you are hiring the average and calling it growth.

Appoint your bar raiser. Someone with no stake in filling the seat, who gets a veto they are allowed to use. Write their name down and tell them. A veto nobody has been told about is not a veto.

Set the stop rule before you start looking. How many candidates you see before you are permitted to say yes, taken from the 37% rule you used in Class 02. Same rule, applied to a person instead of a market.

Write the falsifying question. The one question whose answer would make you drop the candidate you already like. Ask it first, while you can still hear the answer.

Then compress all of it into one line for the Class 12 deck: we hire this person to do this thing, which moves this number. That line is what the money buys, and a room full of investors reads it as the whole hiring plan.

You are deciding what standard your company will hold for the next five years. Ask a model to write your job description and it will hand you the average of every job description ever posted, which is the exact thing the bar raiser exists to stop.

Reading
Paul Graham · How to Raise Money

Money is hiring, mostly. Read it before Class 11.

Deep dive: the right kind of ambition optional

Horowitz splits ambition in two. Some people are ambitious for the company and some are ambitious for themselves, and both types interview well, because the second type has had more practice.

The difference shows up in small places. Watch who they credit when a project goes well and who they blame when it does not. Watch whether they build a team stronger than themselves or one that makes them look necessary. Watch what they do in a meeting where a decision is going the wrong way and taking their side would cost them nothing.

The test is cheap to run and hard to fake over time, which is why references from people who reported to the candidate are worth more than references from people they reported to.
