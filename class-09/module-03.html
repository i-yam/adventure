Three channels, one budget

09
INTEREST sales / COMFORT no-code / TIME 15min / TRACK core / FEEDS class 12 pitch
Three
channels

A promising new channel returns four customers on a small test. So does a dead one.

That is the trouble with channel data. The sample is thin, the variance is wide, and the founder reads the first result as a verdict. Class 02 called this reasoning from little data. Here it arrives with money attached and a team waiting for a decision.

Explore, then exploit. Early, with runway ahead of you, the money spreads out because what you are buying is information. Later it concentrates on whatever survived. Founders reliably run this backwards, betting everything early on the channel they personally enjoy, then exploring in month eighteen when exploring has become unaffordable.

The work
[ NO AI ] DO THIS ONE YOURSELF

Take a real budget. Whatever you could actually spend this month, even if it is three hundred euros. Name three channels you could start on Monday, and split the money across them in writing before anything moves.

Now the part everyone skips. For each channel write the number of conversions you need before the result counts as evidence at all, and the fully loaded CAC above which you stop. Both numbers get set today, while you have no data and therefore no feelings about it.

You leave with an allocation, a kill threshold per channel, a minimum conversion count per channel, and today's date on the page.

AI off. A model will hand you a threshold in four seconds. The point is being the person who committed to one before the money moved.

Deep dive: the metric you are about to break optional

Goodhart's law says a measure stops being a good measure once it becomes a target. Set the team a cost per lead and you will get cheap leads, in volume, that never buy anything. Set them signups and you will get free accounts that log in once. Nobody cheats. Everyone optimises exactly what you asked for, which is the problem.

The defence is to pair the target with the metric it would have to damage. Cost per lead sits next to lead to paid conversion. Signups sit next to week four retention. When one moves and the other collapses, you have your answer about what the team was actually doing.
